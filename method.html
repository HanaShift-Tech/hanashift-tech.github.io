Our Method

We don't translate sentences. We choose the relationship the sentence implies — and then we show you the reasoning.

THE CORE IDEA

One sentence, three relationships

Below is the same English message — "Confirming Tuesday's meeting. I'll send the documents." — written for three different readers. Nothing about the content changes. Everything about the verbs does.

"Confirming Tuesday's meeting. I'll send the documents."
Senior clientOutside your company, above you
いつもお世話になっております。火曜日の会議の件、承知いたしました。関連資料につきましては、本日中に送付させていただきます。

Sonkeigo + kenjōgo. 承知いたしました lowers your own action in front of the reader. 送付させていただきます literally asks permission to send — excessive in English, standard here.

ColleagueInside your company, same level
お疲れ様です。火曜日の会議、了解しました。資料は今日中に送ります。

Teineigo. The opening switches to the internal お疲れ様です. 了解しました, which would have been curt to the client, is correct between peers — and the humble forms would now sound sarcastic.

SupplierOutside your company, below you
いつもお世話になっております。火曜日の会議の件、承知いたしました。資料は本日中にお送りいたします。

Moderate keigo. Politeness towards outsiders stays, but asking permission to act would misread the negotiating position. お送りいたします is courteous without submitting.

THE THREE REGISTERS

What the system is actually choosing between

尊敬語

Sonkeigo — respectful

Raises the other person's actions. Used when you describe what your client, your superior or a guest does. Applying it to your own actions is a well-known and immediately visible mistake.

謙譲語

Kenjōgo — humble

Lowers your own actions relative to the listener. This is where most of the work happens in business email, and where automatic translation almost never goes.

丁寧語

Teineigo — polite

The neutral polite layer — the ～です／～ます forms taught first to learners. Correct almost everywhere, and exactly for that reason too flat where a relationship needs marking.

HOW IT WORKS

One model, specialised per register

Choosing a register is not a bigger-model problem. It is a data problem — which is where most of our work goes.

An open base model, specialised

We start from an open-weight, commercially licensed model and specialise it with lightweight adapters — one per register — rather than maintaining several separate models. One base model, and a register that switches in a few megabytes.

The explanation is part of the output

The model returns the corrected phrasing and the reason for it: which form was used, and what it signals about the relationship. Over a few weeks the user stops needing the tool for routine messages — which is the point.

How we handle your text

Messages are processed on our servers to generate the reply, encrypted in transit and at rest. We do not use customer correspondence to train our models unless you explicitly opt in, and you can delete your history at any time.

Validated by native speakers

Our training pairs are reviewed by native speakers with business experience, because a confidently wrong explanation teaches the user something harmful — worse than giving no answer at all.
