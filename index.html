WHY IT MATTERS

Translation gets the words right and the relationship wrong

Japanese business language encodes the relationship between the two people speaking. Pick the wrong register and a correct sentence still damages the deal — and nothing in the output tells you it happened.

The register problem

The same English sentence has no single Japanese equivalent. Who reads it, and how far above or below you they sit, changes the verb forms. Machine translation flattens all of that into one neutral output.

The subtext problem

A polite refusal in Japanese looks like enthusiasm once translated. Deals stall for months while the European side waits for an answer that has, in effect, already been given.

The cost of getting help

The usual fix is a cultural consultant or a bilingual hire. Both are expensive, slow to reach for a single email, and leave no knowledge behind in the team.
